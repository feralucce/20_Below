Combat Flow Reference

A plain-language walkthrough of how a fight actually plays out, step by step, with the exact roll for each part. See rules.md for the full rules text.

1

Initiative

At the start of the fight, everyone rolls once to see who acts first. This order holds for the entire fight - you don't re-roll each round.

1d10 + Initiative sub-stat
2

Declare Action Bracket

Each round, before anyone acts, every character quietly picks how they're playing it this round: Fast, Normal, or Slow. This decides how many actions you get and when you act relative to everyone else.

Fast
1 action
A snap shot, a move, one Skill use. Acts first, but that's all you get.
Normal
2 actions
Move and attack, for example. Acts second - the balanced middle.
Slow
1 action, empowered
Acts last, but that one action allows called shots and gets Advantage.
3

Resolve Actions

Once everyone's declared, the round actually plays out in three passes:

  1. All Fast actions happen first, in Initiative order.
  2. Then all Normal actions, in Initiative order.
  3. Then all Slow actions, in Initiative order.

Spending 1 Ki bumps your bracket up one step (Slow → Normal, or Normal → Fast); 2 Ki bumps it up two steps at once.

How Attacks Work

A

Do You Even Hit?

Before anything else, you have to actually land the hit. This works the same whether you're swinging a fist, saying something cutting, or pushing into someone's mind - roll your Attribute + Skill against how hard the target is to hit.

Attribute + Skill vs. target's Defense

Roll 2d10 and add them up. If your total is equal to or less than your target number, you hit. Higher than that, and it's a miss - nothing happens this time.

B

How Much Gets Through?

Now roll a handful of dice - how many depends on what you're using (a weapon, a Gift, whatever fits the attack). Check each die one at a time against the target's toughness for that kind of attack: Soak for a physical hit, Presence for words, Psyche for a mental push.

Each die vs. Soak / Presence / Psyche

A die higher than that number gets through. A die equal to or lower just bounces off, no effect. Each connecting die costs the target one Health Level, Poise, or Sanity - whichever fits the attack.

Spending 1 Ki per die lets you boost that die, adding your own Ferocity (Physical), Presence (Social), or Psyche (Mental) to its result - decided before any dice are rolled, not after seeing a bad one. Enough boosted dice can crack through even a maxed-out wall.

How Damage Works

C

Health Levels (Physical)

Think of Health Levels as a row of boxes, not one big number - each box takes exactly one hit before it's gone. Everyone starts with 5, plus whatever they've put into their Health sub-stat.

5 + Health sub-stat

At 0, you're unconscious and can't act. Go far enough past that and you die. One attack can never wipe out more boxes than it takes to reach 0 - even a huge hit just lands you exactly at 0, no further. Once you're already at 0, any later attack can only take one more box, no matter how many dice connect.

Spending 1 Ki cancels the loss of one Health Level - 1 Ki per Level saved, and you can spend more than one at once. Dropping to 0 anyway leaves a permanent scar. See Scars.

D

Poise (Social)

Poise is also a row of boxes, one hit each, starting at 5 plus your Presence sub-stat - but it tracks your composure under Social attack instead of physical harm: cutting words, humiliation, pressure.

5 + Presence

The one difference: Poise can't go below 0. Once you're there, you're as Flustered as it gets - stuck at Disadvantage - but nothing worse happens after that.

Spending 1 Ki cancels the loss of one Poise Level, same rate as Health - 1 Ki per Level saved.

E

Sanity (Mental)

Sanity is also a row of boxes, one hit each, starting at 5 plus your Psyche sub-stat - tracking your grip on your own mind under Mental attack.

5 + Psyche

Unlike Poise, Sanity can drop below 0. At 0 you're Overwhelmed - a temporary bad mental trait, Disadvantage, but you can still act on your own. Push it below 0 and you're Shattered - you can't act alone anymore, someone has to lead you. Dropping below 0 always leaves a lasting mental scar, even after you recover.

Spending 1 Ki cancels the loss of one Sanity Level, same rate as Health. See Scars.

Healing Damage

F

Health Levels

Rest is the normal way back. A short breather patches up some of your boxes based on your Health sub-stat; a full night's sleep patches up every box you've lost.

Short Rest: Health sub-stat ÷ 2 (round up) · Full Night's Rest: full

If you're already below 0, healing is slower - 1 Health Level per day of rest, until you're back up to 0. Normal recovery picks up again from there.

G

Poise

A short breather patches up some of your lost Poise based on your Presence; a full night's sleep patches up all of it.

Short Rest: Presence ÷ 2 (round up) · Full Night's Rest: full
H

Sanity

A short breather patches up some of your lost Sanity based on your Psyche; a full night's sleep patches up all of it.

Short Rest: Psyche ÷ 2 (round up) · Full Night's Rest: full

Overwhelmed (at 0) clears once you're away from whatever caused it and get a chance to rest - or by spending 1 Ki, which also fills Sanity back to full. Shattered (below 0) clears via the same two triggers - removal from the stimulus plus rest, or spending 1 Ki - but either one only refills you to 1, not full; normal recovery continues from there.

I

Ki

A short breather refills some of your spent Ki based on your Klotho; a full night's sleep refills it completely.

Short Rest: Klotho ÷ 2 (round up) · Full Night's Rest: full

Two Gifts can speed any of this up: Healing lets someone else mend you faster, and Regeneration does the same thing to yourself.
