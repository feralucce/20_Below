Part 1 · Lesson 1

20 Below in one paragraph

Say it the same way every time

Everyone on the team describes 20 Below a little differently. In a pitch, that's a problem: the writer needs one clear picture, and the facts must match the press kit they'll open next.

The short description

20 Below is a fated-heroes tabletop RPG: a 2d10 roll-under core, five elemental Attributes, and supernatural Gifts layered over whatever world you bring to it. The core rules are free forever under the ORC License, and so is its first full setting, Backwater Static, a supernatural New Orleans built on secrecy and static.

It's on the press kit page, with a Copy button, alongside the one-line and long versions.

Facts you can always use

Free means free

The rules, Backwater Static, the PDFs and every tool cost nothing, under the ORC License. Only print will cost money. Never call anything digital a purchase.

Print

"Print editions are planned." No dates, prices or campaign plans until the team announces them.

The team

Game designers and communications, language and engineering specialists, with a group of playtesters, working on it since March 2021. Never "a solo project".

Where it stands

"In active playtest", not "finished".

The hook

"2d10 roll-under fated heroes, and a free supernatural New Orleans setting."

Part 1 · Lesson 2

Who to pitch

A small game gets covered by people whose audiences look for new systems. The outlet list in the marketing plan skips the biggest names on purpose: they don't take unsolicited system pitches. Start with the list, and check the #press-outreach forum before you pitch anyone on it.

Five kinds of outlet, and what to offer each

Actual-play shows

They need a game to run. Offer a ready one-shot, pregenerated characters and the Owlbear Rodeo extensions.

Indie reviewers

Offer the free rules, the playtest context and a short note on what makes the system different.

Press and newsletters

A short press release, or a link and a blurb. Rascal News is the first send for playtest announcements.

Social creators

A direct, personal message about one thing they'd like, sent through the route they ask for.

New Orleans

Backwater Static's New Orleans opens doors outside RPG media. Lead with the setting and the team.

What do you offer?

A weekly podcast that plays a new system every month.

A YouTube channel that reviews independent RPGs.

A weekly indie RPG newsletter.

A horror actual-play show.

A weekly RPG news roundup.

A reviewer who covers investigative horror games.

Part 2 · Lesson 3

Writing the email

A pitch is read in ten seconds. If the first two lines don't say what it is and why this outlet should care, the rest isn't read.

The pitch, line by line

Short, personal and one ask. Follow the outlet's own submission rules exactly, and never pitch anyone whose page says they aren't taking submissions.

  1. Subject lineWhat it is and the hook, in under eight words: "Free fated-heroes RPG, tools included."
  2. Their name and why themName the person and one thing of theirs you liked. No "Dear Sir or Madam".
  3. What it isThe short description from Lesson 1, word for word.
  4. The askOne thing, matched to their format: a one-shot on the show, a look at the rules, a mention. Never offer or imply payment, and never ask for a positive review.
  5. Links, not attachmentsThe press kit (20belowrpg.com/press.html) and a direct free link to the rules, with no signup. Mention that the Character Creator and Owlbear extensions cut prep time.
  6. Sign-off and sendYour name and your role on the team. Send the draft to Feral, then send it from 20belowrpg@gmail.com once approved.

Part 2 · Lesson 4

Timing and the press kit

Pitch early

Shows book months ahead. Pitch two to three months before anything you want covered, like a playtest round or a release, and say up front if there's an embargo.

Every pitch links to the press kit at 20belowrpg.com/press.html, so the writer never has to ask for an image. Here's what's on it:

  1. One-line, short and long descriptions, each with a Copy button
  2. A fact sheet: type, mechanic, license, price, first setting, status and start date
  3. What's free today, tool by tool
  4. The mark for dark and light backgrounds, cover art, Backwater Static art and tool screenshots
  5. The team, the links and the press contact

Part 3 · Lesson 5

Follow up and log it

Every contact is logged in the #press-outreach forum on Discord, so nobody on the team pitches the same person twice. Search it before you draft a pitch.

  1. Log itOne post per outlet, titled "Outlet · Person", tagged Pitched. Note the date, what you asked for and how you contacted them.
  2. Wait about three weeksThen send one short follow-up in the same email thread, and change the tag to Followed up.
  3. Then stopNo reply to the follow-up means not now. Tag it No reply and move on. Never follow up twice, and never mass-email.
  4. If they say yesAnswer within 48 hours and tag it Yes. When the piece runs, tag it Covered and link it in the thread.

One follow-up. Then let it go.

Part 3 · Lesson 6

Rules of the road

What we never do

Five lines no 20 Below pitch crosses. Open each one to see what to do instead.

Promise dates or prices

Say "print editions are planned". Dates come from the team, in the newsletter.

Pay, or hint at paying

Use each outlet's free route. If their only route is paid promotion, skip them.

Ask for a positive review

Ask them to take a look. What they say is theirs.

Hide who we are

Posting about 20 Below in a community? Say you're on the team, and follow that community's self-promotion rules.

Talk down other games

Pitch what 20 Below does, not what others don't.

When coverage lands

Thank them the same day, and share it everywhere: the 20 Below socials, the Discord, and the team for the newsletter. That sharing is the payback creators expect. Tag the post Covered with the link.
