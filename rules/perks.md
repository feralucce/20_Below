# Perks

## What a Perk Is

Perks are a **grab bag** — small advantages that don't fit under Skills (learned, practiced competence) or Gifts (truly exceptional/supernatural capability). Unlike everything else in the system, Perks:

- **Have no levels or ranks.** A character either has a Perk or doesn't — there's no 1-10 scale, no dots, no tiers.
- **Do not grant numerical bonuses in most cases.** A Perk is usually a rule exception, an unlocked narrative option, or a standing fact about the character's resources/reputation/body — not a flat +N to some roll. (A handful of edge cases may genuinely need a small numeric effect; treat those as rare exceptions, not the norm — flagged below where relevant.)

Perks have a mirror-image counterpart: **[Flaws](flaws.md)**, character-creation disadvantages built the same way (no levels, mostly non-numeric).

## Sources Reviewed

Compiled and reorganized from three very different traditions, deduplicated:

- **Old World of Darkness (Merits)** — mundane and supernatural advantages, normally dotted 1-5; we strip the dots.
- **Hero System (Perks & Talents)** — Hero splits these into two categories (Perks = resources/social standing like Contacts, Fringe Benefits, Wealth; Talents = innate quirks like Ambidexterity, Eidetic Memory). Since our system has one unified "Perk" bucket, both contribute.
- **D&D Feats (3e / 3.5e / 5e)** — many D&D feats grant flat numeric bonuses (Weapon Focus, Great Weapon Master) or are inherently magical (Ritual Caster, Elemental Adept). Numeric-bonus feats and magical feats were **left out** — they don't fit "no numeric bonus, not a power." What's included below are the feats whose core idea is a rule exception or unlocked capability rather than a number.

Overtly supernatural material from any of these three sources (spirit mentors, magical bloodlines, elemental affinities, etc.) was excluded here — those belong under **Gifts**, not Perks, per the "truly exceptional" vs. "small advantage" split.

## Perk List

### Physical

| Perk | Effect |
|---|---|
| Ambidextrous | No penalty for using your off-hand |
| Double-Jointed | Escape bonds/tight spaces, contort with ease |
| Iron Stomach | Eat or drink almost anything without illness |
| Light Sleeper | Wake instantly at the slightest disturbance |
| Fleet of Foot | Noticeably faster on foot than the effort should allow |
| Unusual Size | Notably larger or smaller than average, with the narrative tradeoffs that implies |

### Mental / Cognitive

| Perk | Effect |
|---|---|
| Eidetic Memory | Perfect recall of anything seen, read, or heard |
| Lightning Calculator | Instant complex mental math |
| Speed Reading | Read or skim at exceptional speed |
| Common Sense | The GM will give you a heads-up before you commit to an obviously terrible decision |
| Concentration | Impervious to being distracted once focused on a task |
| Iron Will | Notably resistant to fear, intimidation, and manipulation |
| Danger Sense | A "bad feeling" before danger strikes — the GM gives you a warning |

### Social

| Perk | Effect |
|---|---|
| Enchanting Voice | Your voice alone holds attention and charms |
| Natural Linguist | Pick up new languages unusually fast |
| Higher Purpose | Others innately sense you're meant for something greater |
| Prestigious Name | An inherited or earned reputation that opens doors before you say a word |
| Contacts | A person (or small network) who reliably helps you when asked |
| Fame | People recognize your name or face and react accordingly — for better or worse |

### Resources & Standing

| Perk | Effect |
|---|---|
| Fringe Benefit | A license, membership, clearance, or legal right most people don't have |
| Base | A fixed headquarters or safehouse you can return to |
| Notable Vehicle | Ownership of a distinctive, capable vehicle |
| Followers | A small group loyal to you |
| Wealth | Meaningfully more disposable resources than an average person *(edge case — may need a light numeric guideline; see Open Questions)* |

### Combat-Adjacent — confirmed

Resolved 2026-07-14, now that combat mechanics exist — see [combat.md](combat.md).

| Perk | Effect |
|---|---|
| Combat Reflexes | **+5 to the Initiative Degree** (not the target number) — applied the same way as any other Gift/Perk Initiative modifier, per the existing convention in [combat.md](combat.md#initiative--confirmed): raw roll and crit status are determined first, off the unmodified target number, then Perk modifiers shift the resulting Degree before ranking. |
| Quick Draw | Readying a weapon costs nothing — normally an Action (see the Action examples in [combat.md](combat.md#action-economy--confirmed)), this Perk makes it free, foldable into the same turn as an attack. |
| Alertness | Cannot be surprised while conscious, unless under a mind-altering effect that would specifically allow it. |

**Flagged**: Alertness is the first thing in the system to reference "surprise" as a concept — combat.md doesn't yet define what being surprised actually does mechanically (no Initiative penalty, no free hit, etc. currently exists). This Perk grants immunity to a condition that itself still needs to be designed.

## Open Questions

- Flat cost to acquire a Perk (points at creation, XP afterward) — same currency as Skills, but is the cost flat across all Perks or does it vary by how strong the Perk is?
- Is there a cap on how many Perks a character can take?
- Do any Perks have prerequisites (e.g. Fringe Benefit requiring an appropriate social position established in the fiction)?
- How to handle the genuine numeric-edge-case Perks (Wealth, and anything found later) without breaking the "no numeric bonus" rule of thumb — a small fixed exception list, or reframe them as pure narrative facts instead?
- **New**: what does "surprised" actually do mechanically? (Raised by Alertness — see above.)
