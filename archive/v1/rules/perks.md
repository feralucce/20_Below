# Perks

## What a Perk Is

Perks are a **grab bag** — small advantages that don't fit under Skills (learned, practiced competence) or Gifts (truly exceptional/supernatural capability). Unlike everything else in the system, most Perks:

- **Have no levels or ranks.** A character either has a Perk or doesn't — there's no 1-10 scale, no dots, no tiers. (**Exception**: a subset of resource-type Perks use a 1-5 level scale instead — see [Leveled (Resource) Perks](#leveled-resource-perks--confirmed) below.)
- **Do not grant numerical bonuses to rolls.** A Perk is usually a rule exception, an unlocked narrative option, or a standing fact about the character's resources/reputation/body — not a flat +N to some roll. **Resource-type Perks (Wealth, Contacts, Fringe Benefit, etc.) are the exception, but not a violation of this rule**: they represent a currency/resource pool the character can spend or lean on narratively, not a bonus added to a roll — see [Resolved](#resolved) below.

Perks have a mirror-image counterpart: **[Flaws](flaws.md)**, character-creation disadvantages built the same way (no levels, mostly non-numeric).

## Sources Reviewed

Compiled and reorganized from three very different traditions, deduplicated:

- **Old World of Darkness (Merits)** — mundane and supernatural advantages, normally dotted 1-5; we strip the dots.
- **Hero System (Perks & Talents)** — Hero splits these into two categories (Perks = resources/social standing like Contacts, Fringe Benefits, Wealth; Talents = innate quirks like Ambidexterity, Eidetic Memory). Since our system has one unified "Perk" bucket, both contribute.
- **D&D Feats (3e / 3.5e / 5e)** — many D&D feats grant flat numeric bonuses (Weapon Focus, Great Weapon Master) or are inherently magical (Ritual Caster, Elemental Adept). Numeric-bonus feats and magical feats were **left out** — they don't fit "no numeric bonus, not a power." What's included below are the feats whose core idea is a rule exception or unlocked capability rather than a number.

Overtly supernatural material from any of these three sources (spirit mentors, magical bloodlines, elemental affinities, etc.) was excluded here — those belong under **Gifts**, not Perks, per the "truly exceptional" vs. "small advantage" split.

### Leveled (Resource) Perks — confirmed

**Resolved 2026-07-14**: these Perks represent a scalable resource rather than a binary yes/no trait, so they break from the normal "no levels" rule and instead use the **same 1-5 level scale as [Gifts](gifts.md)** — each level a cumulative, defined step up in scope (Discipline-style), not a numeric roll bonus. Moved here from Social/Resources & Standing since the leveled structure applies to all of them.

| Perk | What Scales by Level |
|---|---|
| Wealth | How much disposable financial resource is available to draw on |
| Fringe Benefit | The reach/exclusivity of the license, membership, clearance, or legal right |
| Contacts | How large, capable, or well-placed the network is |
| Base | How developed, defended, or well-equipped the headquarters/safehouse is |
| Notable Vehicle | How distinctive, capable, or valuable the vehicle is |
| Followers | How many, how skilled, or how loyal the group is |
| Fame | How widely recognized the character is, and how strongly people react to it |

**Per-level content, confirmed 2026-07-15** — each level is a cumulative, defined step up in scope, same convention as the Gift catalog's own level tables ([premade-gifts.md](premade-gifts.md)):

| Level | Wealth | Fringe Benefit | Contacts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paycheck-to-paycheck; covers rent and food, nothing extra | A basic hobbyist license/membership | One reliable contact for small favors or info |
| 2 | Comfortable; can afford occasional splurges without strain | A recognized professional credential (licensed trade, press pass) | A handful of contacts spanning a couple of fields |
| 3 | Well-off; owns property outright, can make major purchases freely | Restricted-access clearance — into secured facilities, government-adjacent circles | A broad network spanning most of a city |
| 4 | Wealthy; can finance a major undertaking outright | High-level clearance — classified access, executive-level standing | An extensive network with regional reach, including a few influential names |
| 5 | A fortune large enough to move markets or bankroll entire operations | Near-total access — opens virtually any door in its domain | A vast network reaching into any relevant field or region, including genuinely powerful people |

| Level | Base | Notable Vehicle | Followers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A modest single room or hideout, minimal security | Reliable but unremarkable | A single loyal follower or apprentice |
| 2 | A proper base with basic amenities and working locks | One standout feature — notably fast, armored, or distinctive | A small handful of modestly capable followers |
| 3 | Well-equipped, with real security and specialized rooms | Significantly customized, high-performance | A coordinated, reasonably capable crew |
| 4 | Fortified and staffed, with serious defenses and advanced equipment | Rare and exceptional — armored, military-grade, or famous | A sizable, well-organized group with real capability |
| 5 | A near-impenetrable, fully self-sufficient stronghold | One-of-a-kind, legendary in capability or reputation | A substantial, highly capable and loyal organization |

| Level | Fame |
|---|---|
| 1 | Known within a small local circle |
| 2 | Recognized across a city or subculture |
| 3 | Known regionally or nationally |
| 4 | Broadly famous — recognized by strangers regularly |
| 5 | World-renowned, a household name |

**Exact per-level definitions, resolved 2026-07-15** — see the level tables above.

### Combat-Adjacent — confirmed

Resolved 2026-07-14, now that combat mechanics exist — see [combat.md](combat.md).

| Perk | Tier | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Combat Reflexes | Major | **+5 to the Initiative Degree** (not the target number) — applied the same way as any other Gift/Perk Initiative modifier, per the existing convention in [combat.md](combat.md#initiative--confirmed): raw roll and crit status are determined first, off the unmodified target number, then Perk modifiers shift the resulting Degree before ranking. |
| Quick Draw | Moderate | Readying a weapon costs nothing — normally an Action (see the Action examples in [combat.md](combat.md#action-economy--confirmed)), this Perk makes it free, foldable into the same turn as an attack. |
| Alertness | Moderate | Immune to being Surprised while conscious, unless under a mind-altering effect that would specifically allow it — see [Surprise](combat.md#surprise--confirmed) for what that immunity actually covers (Advantage to attackers, -5 Initiative Degree for the first round). |

## Resolved

- **What "Surprised" does mechanically, resolved** (2026-07-14): Advantage to attacks against the surprised character, plus -5 to their Initiative Degree for the first round of combat. See [Surprise](combat.md#surprise--confirmed) — Alertness's immunity now has a defined effect to be immune to.
- **Cost scales with Perk strength** (2026-07-14): not a flat cost across all Perks — a bigger rule exception or a higher-scope resource costs more than a minor one.
- **Individual Perk costs within the 10-point creation Perk pool, confirmed 2026-07-14**: a **3-tier system**, GM/player negotiated per Perk the same way Flaw severity is negotiated —

  | Tier | Cost | Examples of fit |
  |---|---|---|
  | Minor | 1 point | Small, mostly-cosmetic rule exceptions (Ambidextrous, Speed Reading) |
  | Moderate | 2 points | A genuinely useful edge with real table impact (Danger Sense, Iron Will) |
  | Major | 3 points | Perks that meaningfully reshape how a character handles a whole category of situation (Combat Reflexes) |

- **Acquiring a new Perk after character creation (XP), confirmed 2026-07-14**: `tier × 3` XP — **Minor 3, Moderate 6, Major 9**. This is the premium multiplier flagged earlier as still-open: a 3× markup over the creation-pool point cost, making buying a Perk mid-campaign meaningfully harder than getting one at creation, without needing separate numbers for every individual Perk.

  Leveled (Resource) Perks use a **separate flat 1 point per level**, matching Gifts' own flat 1:1 convention — a level-3 Wealth costs 3 points from the Perk pool. (This is the *creation-pool* cost; the Wildcard Point cost of 3/Perk resolved earlier in [character-creation/overview.md](../character-creation/overview.md#calibration) is a different, already-settled number for a different currency.) With a 10-point pool, this supports anywhere from ~10 Minor Perks to ~3 Major ones, or any mix — deliberately not prescriptive about which combination a character should take.
- **No cap on Perks** (2026-07-14), but **acquiring a new Perk after character creation costs a premium** over the equivalent creation-time cost — buying one during play should be the harder path, not equally easy. Exact premium multiplier TBD alongside the cost numbers above.
- **No prerequisites** (2026-07-14): a Perk grants its own standing rather than requiring it already exist in the fiction — e.g. taking Fringe Benefit is what creates the license/membership/clearance, not a reward for already having it.
- **Numeric edge cases resolved**: Wealth and the other Leveled (Resource) Perks don't violate the "no numeric bonus" rule, because they aren't a bonus applied to a roll — they're a **resource/currency pool** the character draws on narratively (and, once designed, mechanically at the table), the same category of thing as the Pre-Rolled Combat Dice bank or Resonance, not a flat +N modifier.
- **Every non-Leveled Perk tagged with a cost tier** (2026-07-15) — see the Tier column added to each table in the Perk List above. Anchored against the three tiers already defined by example (Minor: Ambidextrous, Speed Reading; Moderate: Danger Sense, Iron Will; Major: Combat Reflexes): Minor perks are narrow/situational/mostly cosmetic (Double-Jointed, Iron Stomach, Light Sleeper, Lightning Calculator, Natural Linguist, Higher Purpose); Moderate perks have recurring, real table impact (Fleet of Foot, Unusual Size, Eidetic Memory, Common Sense, Concentration, Enchanting Voice, Prestigious Name, Quick Draw, Alertness); no additional Perk was judged to reach Major — Combat Reflexes remains the sole example of a Perk that reshapes a whole category of play on its own. Leveled (Resource) Perks (Wealth, Contacts, etc.) are excluded from this tiering — they already use their own flat 1 point/level cost, not the 3-tier system.
- **Per-level content for all 7 Leveled (Resource) Perks, resolved** (2026-07-15) — see the level tables under [Leveled (Resource) Perks](#leveled-resource-perks--confirmed) above. Same "cumulative, defined step up in scope" convention as the Gift catalog's own level tables, calibrated against WoD Background level content (Resources/Contacts/Allies/Backup/Fame) already cited elsewhere in this project's research.
