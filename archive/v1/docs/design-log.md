# Design Log

Running log of decisions, rationale, and open questions as the system is built.

## Decisions

- **2026-07-11** — Three base attributes: Body (Physical), Mind (Mental), Soul. Scored 1-10.
- **2026-07-11** — Three tiers of character capability: Skills (learned, 1-10), Perks (minor advantages, scale TBD), Gifts (exceptional abilities, 1-10).
- **2026-07-11** — Core resolution: Attribute + secondary score = target number (1-20). Roll 1d20, succeed on target or lower ("roll under").
- **2026-07-11** — Combined modifier approach chosen over a single system:
  - Flat modifiers (-5 to +5) are the default GM tool for stacking situational bonuses/penalties. Linear, 5% per point.
  - Advantage (2d20 take lower) / Disadvantage (2d20 take higher) is a separate, non-stacking binary tool reserved for specific triggers rather than everyday use.
- **2026-07-11** — Modified target number is clamped to 1-20, but natural 1 always critically succeeds and natural 20 always critically fails, regardless of the modified target ("always live" extremes).
- **2026-07-11** — Saved a full probability reference table (target 1-20 x modifier -5 to +5) for player reference — see [probability-reference.md](../rules/probability-reference.md).
- **2026-07-11** — Skills are not attribute-locked: any skill can pair with any attribute if the player can argue the pairing to the GM's satisfaction (see [skills.md](../rules/skills.md)).
- **2026-07-11** — No fixed master skill list — premades will be provided but players/GMs are encouraged to invent skills freely.
- **2026-07-11** — Specialties: 1-2 word descriptors narrowing a skill, granted at skill rank 6 (1st), 8 (2nd), 10 (3rd). Using an applicable specialty grants Advantage on the roll.
- **2026-07-11** — Critical success/failure outside combat: pure auto-succeed/auto-fail, with any extra effect narrated by the GM in the moment (no fixed table) — reflects the system's design focus on social interaction and GM authority over the fiction.
- **2026-07-11** — Combat gets fixed critical effects instead of pure GM narration: critical success = double damage + GM-narrated extra effect; critical failure effect TBD; called shots impose a difficulty modifier (value TBD). Full combat design deferred until after the skill system is finished — see [combat.md](../rules/combat.md).
- **2026-07-11** — Compiled a premade skill list from OWoD, Pathfinder 1e, and Call of Cthulhu 7e (Amber Diceless has no skill list to draw from) — see [premade-skills.md](../rules/premade-skills.md).
- **2026-07-11** — Combat skills (Melee, Unarmed, Firearms, Archery/Thrown, Heavy Weapons, Dodge) set aside in their own section pending combat mechanics design.
- **2026-07-11** — Skills stay **broad** by default (one "Science" skill, not split by discipline) rather than narrow. Reasoning: specialties already narrow a broad skill down to a specific application, and since specialties can also be purchased outright (not just earned at rank 6/8/10), broad skills don't lose precision.
- **2026-07-11** — Character creation is a **point-based build system**; advancement afterward is **direct XP expenditure** (no classes or levels, no fixed progression track) — players build and grow characters exactly how they want. Resolves the specialty-purchase currency question: extra specialties are bought with XP like anything else. See [character-creation/overview.md](../character-creation/overview.md).
- **2026-07-11** — Skill system finalized (see [skills.md](../rules/skills.md)):
  - Skill+attribute pairings are re-argued every time, not banked as precedent — justification is situational, not a permanent character fact.
  - New skills cannot be created mid-session; skill creation happens between sessions, purchased with XP, subject to GM discussion and approval (existence, scope, and cost all included).
  - Specialties' entire mechanical effect is Advantage — nothing else, no flat bonus or auto-success.
- **2026-07-11** — Perks defined (see [perks.md](../rules/perks.md)): a grab-bag category for small advantages that aren't Skills or Gifts. No levels/ranks — a character has a Perk or doesn't. No numeric bonuses in most cases; usually a rule exception or unlocked narrative option. Compiled from OWoD Merits, Hero System Perks & Talents, and non-magical/non-numeric D&D feats (3e/3.5e/5e) — overtly supernatural material from any source was routed to Gifts instead, and combat-adjacent Perks were set aside pending combat design, same as combat skills.
- **2026-07-11** — Gifts corrected from 1-10 to **1-5**, World of Darkness Discipline-style: each Gift is unique/narrow in scope (unlike broad Skills), and each of its 5 levels is a specific, cumulative, pre-defined capability that must be unlocked in order. Creating a new Gift is possible but requires an intensive GM discussion (heavier scrutiny than new Skills) covering scope, all 5 levels, thematic fit, and any drawbacks. See [gifts.md](../rules/gifts.md).
- **2026-07-11** — Researched power resolution across Hero System, OWoD, D&D feats/spells, Mutants & Masterminds, Trinity, and Aberrant — found a fork between "user rolls, target resists on their own terms" (OWoD/Trinity/Aberrant/Hero) vs. "power sets a static DC, target rolls against it" (D&D/M&M). Went with the former.
- **2026-07-11** — Built a **Resisted Roll** mechanic in [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#resisted-rolls), usable for any opposed roll (not just Gifts): both sides roll normally against their own target number, each computes `margin = target − die result`, and higher margin wins regardless of technical success/failure. Crits override margin (nat 1 beats anything but another nat 1; nat 20 loses to anything but another nat 20). Ties, including matching crits, default to the defender winning.
- **2026-07-11** — **Superseded**: Gift resolution is *not* Attribute + Gift Level. A new statistic, placeholder-named **Power Level**, was introduced — inspired by Mage: The Ascension's Arete (a single potency stat separate from the Spheres/Gifts that define what can be affected). Gifts govern WHAT can be done; Power Level governs HOW POWERFUL a character is at using Gifts in general. Using a Gift rolls **Power Level + Body/Mind/Soul** (attribute chosen situationally, same negotiation as Skill+Attribute pairing). See [gifts.md](../rules/gifts.md).
- **2026-07-11** — Resolved: a Gift's own Level (1-5) governs **scope, not magnitude** — a pure gate on what a character can attempt, with zero numeric weight in the roll. Magnitude is entirely down to the Power Level + Attribute roll. Power Level is scored **1-10**, same scale as Body/Mind/Soul.
- **2026-07-11** — Compiled premade Gift source material and worked examples from Changeling: The Dreaming, Werewolf: The Apocalypse, Mage: The Ascension, Wraith: The Oblivion, and Hunter: The Reckoning — see [premade-gifts.md](../rules/premade-gifts.md). Includes 3 fully worked example Gifts (Ember Touch, Skin of the Beast, Beyond the Veil), each with all 5 levels defined.
- **2026-07-11** — Resolved: **Power Level is its own independently-assigned statistic**, not a figured/derived characteristic calculated from Body, Mind, Soul, or any combination of them — bought and advanced on its own, same as an Attribute. Documented as a dedicated section in [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#power-level).
- **2026-07-11** — Introduced **Flaws** (see [flaws.md](../rules/flaws.md)): the mirror image of Perks, disadvantages inspired by White Wolf's Merits & Flaws. Same structure as Perks (no levels, mostly non-numeric), but taken only at character creation, presumably in exchange for something in return (economy TBD).
- **2026-07-11** — Expanded [premade-gifts.md](../rules/premade-gifts.md) into a comprehensive out-of-the-box power catalog: added Exalted, Adventure!, Pendragon, Scion, In Nomine, Hero System, and Heroes Unlimited as further sources, plus general comic book/anime/fantasy/sci-fi convention. Result is ~50 Gift concepts across 13 themed categories (Physical, Elemental, Mental/Psychic, Biological, Social, Movement, Temporal, Fate/Luck, Light/Darkness, Sound, Life/Death, Tech/Bio-Tech, Cosmic/Divine) plus 3 additional fully worked examples (Storm's Herald, Chorus of Concord, Unerring Hand) — 6 total.
- **2026-07-13** — Cleared a batch of small Combat loose ends:
  - **Grapple's attacker Skill is Unarmed Combat** — picked from the existing premade list rather than inventing a new Skill.
  - **"Standard NPC" vs. "Named NPC/PC" formally defined**, resolving the distinction introduced by Catastrophic Head: Standard NPCs are disposable rogues'-gallery/bestiary threats with no special mechanical access; Named NPCs (BBEGs, significant henchmen) use the same mechanisms as PCs **except Spark and forcing dice from the bank**, which stay player-only.
  - **Combat's skill+attribute pairing confirmed to follow the same free/situational rule as every other Skill** — no combat-specific lock-in (e.g. a feinting melee attack could argue Mind instead of Body, subject to GM approval like any pairing).
  - **The 6 combat Skills (Melee, Unarmed, Firearms, Archery/Thrown, Heavy Weapons, Dodge) are finalized**, no longer flagged "deferred," in [premade-skills.md](../rules/premade-skills.md#combat--confirmed).
  - **"Defense halved (round up)" confirmed** as Charge/Brace's cost, no longer just proposed.
  - **A wounded character's Defense does NOT degrade under Health Level penalties** — Defense stays level; only the flat roll penalty applies to actual rolls. Reasoning given: stacking a Defense death-spiral on top of the existing roll penalty would be too crippling ("otherwise characters would be fucked").
  - **Incapacitated lethality partially resolved**: "any further damage kills" now requires the damage be **intentional** (an attacker specifically trying to kill them) — incidental damage no longer auto-kills an Incapacitated character. **First Aid introduced as the first piece of the Healing mechanism** (previously entirely undesigned): a First Aid Skill roll restores HP equal to DoS, capped so it cannot raise the target above the Suffering Health Level — full recovery still requires something beyond field first aid, not yet designed. See [premade-skills.md](../rules/premade-skills.md#medical) and [combat.md](../rules/combat.md#resolved-2026-07-13).
- **2026-07-13** — **Combat Critical Failure resolved**, mirroring the Called Shot pattern: a category × severity-tier table instead of a single flat effect. Adapted a large system-neutral reference list (categories inspired by Rolemaster/Mythras/WFRP/Cyberpunk RED-style crit-fail tables), trimming heavily — kept Weapon Mishap, Self-Injury, Positional Failure, Defensive Failure, and Team Mishap (friendly fire); explicitly excluded the Social and per-Skill fumble tables (Athletics, Stealth, Thievery, etc.) and Magical Mishaps, since only combat gets a fixed crit-fail table by existing design — everything else stays pure GM narration. **Severity is keyed off `|DoF|` (Degree of Failure)**, same tier bands as Called Shot's DoS tiers (0-2/3-5/6-8/9+), for consistency between the two systems. **Simplified `DoF = target − 20` always** — even for a Multiple Actions chain's expanded crit-fail range (which can trigger on a roll below 20), rather than computing it off whichever specific die actually triggered the fail. Effects reuse only mechanics the system already has (Disadvantage, Prone, lose Move/Action, Defense-halved/Defense-0 conventions borrowed from Charge/Brace and All-Out Attack/Haymaker, and the Damage formula itself for self-injury/friendly-fire). See [combat.md](../rules/combat.md#critical-failure--confirmed).
- **2026-07-13** — **Called Shot location effects confirmed**, tiered by severity instead of a flat single effect. Researched several systems' critical-injury tables (Rolemaster, Mythras, WFRP, Cyberpunk RED, Fallout, Dragonbane) for the general "minor → moderate → severe → catastrophic" shape, then adapted rather than imported wholesale — final tables reuse only mechanics the system already has (Disadvantage, Prone, lose Move/Action, Speed reduction, Disarmed, Health Levels/Incapacitated), skipping subsystems this game doesn't have (attribute drain, exhaustion tracks, wound-specific bleed-over-time). **Severity tier is keyed off leftover DoS (`DoS − Defense`)** — the same number that already drives bonus Damage — so no new roll or tracked number was added. Head/Arms-Hands/Legs-Feet each got a 4-tier table. Also **corrected the crit-damage rule**: on a critical success, only the Weapon Base doubles (`(Weapon Base × 2) + (DoS − Defense)`), not the whole total — doubling the already-unbounded leftover-DoS term on top of a guaranteed hit was judged too swingy. **Catastrophic Head** got a special case: outright kill on a "Standard NPC" (disposable/mook-tier threat), but on a "Named NPC" or PC it instead drains the current Health Level and spills into the next (added to the Ordnance/Siege overflow exception from the Damage decision above) — explicitly *not* an autokill beyond the game's normal Incapacitated rule. This introduces a new Standard-vs-Named NPC distinction that doesn't exist anywhere else in the system yet; left as an informal GM judgment call, flagged as an open TODO to formalize later. See [combat.md](../rules/combat.md#special-maneuvers--confirmed).
- **2026-07-13** — Removed **Eyes** as a Called Shot location (was: Blinded, Disadvantage on sight-based actions until treated). Called Shot locations are now Head, Arms/Hands, Legs/Feet.
- **2026-07-13** — **Damage resolved**: `Weapon Base + (DoS − Defense)`, doubled on a critical success, no separate damage roll — reuses the attack roll's own DoS (continuing the "DoS drives magnitude" theme) rather than adding a second, non-d20 roll that wouldn't fit the Pre-Rolled Combat Dice bank. Weapon Base is a **flat integer, 1-10**, assigned by a 10-tier weapon class ladder (Unarmed, Improvised, Light, Simple, Medium, Martial, Heavy, Superheavy, Ordnance, Siege/Cataclysmic) rather than a per-weapon catalog — chosen to match the system's existing universal 1-10 Attribute/Skill scale. Researched real-world lethality first: trauma data shows gunshot wounds run roughly 4x more lethal than blade wounds by mortality rate, but the system deliberately chose **not** to model that gap as extra damage — melee and ranged weapons of comparable "weight class" (dagger/pistol, sword/SMG, greatsword/battle rifle, etc.) share the same base, and firearms' real advantage is represented as **range** instead, which the combat system doesn't model numerically yet. Calibrated against an average character (Body 5, Mind 5 → Defense 3, Healthy pool 10 HP): low tiers average ~2 hits to drain Healthy, top tiers approach a 1-hit drain. Added a **carve-out to the "No Overflow" Health Level rule** (previously absolute, see 2026-07-12 entry below): only the top two tiers, Ordnance (+9) and Siege/Cataclysmic (+10) — heavy weapons, anti-materiel rifles, explosives, mounted/vehicle weapons — are allowed to spill excess damage into subsequent Health Levels in one hit; every other weapon class stays capped at the current container. See [combat.md](../rules/combat.md#damage--confirmed).
- **2026-07-13** — **Superseded: "ties go to the defender" → "ties go to the aggressor,"** system-wide (Resisted Rolls, and the Defense/Resolve hit threshold, which now reads `DoS ≥ Defense/Resolve` instead of strict `>`). Raised while designing Damage: capping weapon damage at a small fixed base plus leftover `DoS − Defense` only works cleanly if a tied roll (DoS == Defense) still counts as a hit with 0 leftover, rather than a miss. Framed as staying consistent with the system's existing unconventional, aggressor-favoring choices — round up (not down) and roll low (not high) — rather than defaulting back to the more common defender-favored convention. See [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#rounding--ties).
- **2026-07-13** — **Movement/Speed resolved**: `Speed = 5m + (Body ÷ 2, round up)`, measured in meters. Deliberately a flat base plus a Body bonus rather than a pure Body-derived formula like Defense/Resolve — a Body-1 character rolling near-zero Speed off a pure-halving formula would be reduced to an unreasonable "3-foot step" per turn, so the 5m base guarantees a normal human walking pace regardless of Body, with the Body term representing the athletic swing on top (range: 6m at Body 1 up to 10m at Body 10). The 5m base itself is anchored to the 3-second round already locked in: D&D's ~30ft/6-second baseline walking pace scales to ~4.5m over 3 seconds, rounded to a clean 5m. Gifts may modify Speed later (flat or multiplicative) — left as an unstarted hook, not designed. See [combat.md](../rules/combat.md#movement--speed--confirmed).
- **2026-07-13** — Three follow-on decisions to Spark and Advantage/Disadvantage:
  - **Spark overflow**: a character who would gain Spark while already holding it (most commonly a GM award mid-session) gains Advantage on their next roll instead, since Spark can't stack.
  - **Advantage/Disadvantage stacking resolved**: superseded the old "doesn't stack, cancels to normal" rule. Now numeric — each source is ±1, all applicable sources sum to a net value, and the net's *sign* (not magnitude) picks Advantage (2d20 take lower), Disadvantage (2d20 take higher), or a normal roll. Three Advantage sources against two Disadvantage sources nets to +1 — an ordinary single Advantage roll, not a stronger one. See [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#advantage--disadvantage--confirmed).
  - **Advantage/Disadvantage vs. the Pre-Rolled Combat Dice bank**: because banked results are known values chosen in advance rather than a fresh gamble, net Advantage lets the character choose between rolling live (2d20 take lower) or just spending one banked die — bypassing the bank's "mandatory until exhausted" rule for that roll. Net Disadvantage removes that choice: the character must roll live (2d20 take higher) even with banked dice remaining, since a known banked value shouldn't be able to dodge a Disadvantaged roll. See [combat.md](../rules/combat.md#pre-rolled-combat-dice--confirmed).
- **2026-07-13** — **Spark** introduced and resolved (see [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#spark--confirmed)): a binary per-player checkbox resource, not itself combat-specific, that answers the combat.md TODO on forcing the GM to use a banked roll. Three spends: (1) hand the GM one of your own bad Pre-Rolled Combat Dice results, forcing them to use it instead of their own roll — declared after the roll attempt but before its effect resolves; (2) grant yourself Advantage on any d20 roll, in or out of combat; (3) gift one of your banked dice to another player. Uses #1 and #3 are combat-only since they depend on the dice bank existing; bank access outside combat is otherwise restricted to Spark spends. Researched D&D 5e Inspiration (binary, GM-awarded for roleplay/heroism/cleverness, ~1/session pace, can be gifted between players) and d20 Modern Action Points (cumulative pool, grows per level, spent as a bonus die) as reference models before deciding the shape. Regain rule: refills at session start (matching the dice bank's own reset), plus the GM can hand it back mid-session for standout play if already spent — the 5e Inspiration model layered on top of the bank's session cadence, not the d20 Modern per-level-growth model. A few edge cases (redirecting a GM award when already full; how Advantage applies to a banked vs. live roll) left open in the doc.
- **2026-07-13** — **Combat Skills revised**: Firearms and Archery/Thrown Weapons merged into one Skill (Firearms/Archery) — the range-vs-lethality distinction is already handled by weapon class in the Damage system, not by separate Skills. Heavy Weapons renamed to **Special Weapons** and rescoped as an exception to the broad-skill norm: it now covers a single specific exotic/siege-class weapon (defined per campaign/character), not a general siege/artillery/vehicle-weapon category. **Dodge/Evasion dropped as a Skill entirely** — avoiding attacks is already handled by the Defense trait, so a separate rolled Skill was redundant. Combat now has 4 Skills instead of 6. See [premade-skills.md](../rules/premade-skills.md#combat--confirmed).
- **2026-07-13** — **Genre-flagged skills resolved**: Occult, Mythos, and Alchemy ship as defaults, with GM discretion to drop any that don't fit a campaign's premise — rather than being opt-in GM-added modules. See [skills.md](../rules/skills.md), [premade-skills.md](../rules/premade-skills.md#combat--confirmed).
- **2026-07-14** — **The 3 combat-adjacent Perks resolved**: Combat Reflexes grants +5 to the Initiative Degree — applied via the existing Gift/Perk-modifies-Degree convention (not the target number), consistent with how Reckless Instinct/Danger Sense already work. Quick Draw makes readying a weapon free instead of costing an Action. Alertness grants immunity to being surprised while conscious, unless a mind-altering effect specifically allows it. Flagged a gap this raised: "surprised" isn't itself a defined mechanical condition anywhere in combat.md yet (no Initiative penalty, no free attack, etc.) — Alertness grants immunity to something that still needs its own design pass. See [perks.md](../rules/perks.md#combat-adjacent--confirmed).
- **2026-07-14** — **Advantage/Disadvantage "full trigger list" resolved as deliberately non-exhaustive**, rather than a closed enumerated list. Compiled an illustrative candidate set by scanning the systems already referenced elsewhere in this project for situational-bonus mechanics: Pathfinder 1e flanking/Aid Another/Inspire Courage, Call of Cthulhu 7e's Bonus Die for right tool/environment, Rifts' strike-from-behind, Exalted/Scion/Adventure!'s stunting (bonus dice for vivid description), Pendragon's Passion-driven rolls, Hero System's positional/teamwork bonuses. "Taking extra time" was considered and explicitly dropped — most source systems treat it as a near-guarantee (Take 20-style), not a probability shift, so it's a poor fit for the Advantage mechanic specifically. Framed the whole list as illustrative, not exclusive, matching the existing GM-discretion "genuinely uncertain situation" trigger already in the doc — the GM can recognize other fitting triggers as they come up. See [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#advantage-trigger-examples--non-exhaustive).
- **2026-07-14** — **Disadvantage trigger list compiled as the mirror of the Advantage list**, same sourcing and same non-exhaustive framing: being flanked/outnumbered, unfavorable terrain, being surprised, missing the right tool or a hostile environment, acting against a character's own Passion/Flaw, and being intimidated/demoralized by an enemy's Leadership/Intimidation use. **Correction**: "rushing" was initially dropped as a poor-fit mirror of "taking extra time," but on review the system already has a mechanism for it — [Multiple Actions](../rules/combat.md#multiple-actions--confirmed) already represents acting fast/rushed via its own per-action target penalty. Reclassified alongside the other already-confirmed mechanical triggers (net Disadvantage vs. the dice bank, Prone, Called Shot/Critical Failure effects) rather than treated as a dropped candidate. See [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#disadvantage-trigger-examples--non-exhaustive).
- **2026-07-14** — **Perk acquisition/scope questions resolved**: cost scales with a Perk's strength rather than a flat rate (exact numbers still deferred to the shared point/XP economy question). No cap on total Perks, but acquiring a new one **after** character creation costs a premium over the creation-time rate — buying one mid-campaign should be the harder path. No prerequisites: a Perk grants its own standing rather than requiring it already exist in the fiction (taking Fringe Benefit is what creates the license, not a reward for already having one). The Wealth-style numeric-edge-case question is resolved by reframing: these Perks are a **resource/currency pool** a character draws on, not a bonus applied to a roll, so they were never actually violating the "no numeric roll bonus" rule in the first place.
- **2026-07-14** — **Addendum: introduced Leveled (Resource) Perks**, a new sub-category breaking the "Perks have no levels" default. Wealth, Fringe Benefit, Contacts, Base, Notable Vehicle, Followers, and Fame all represent a scalable resource rather than a binary trait, so they now use the same **1-5 level scale as Gifts** instead. Moved Contacts and Fame out of the Social list to sit with the rest. Exact per-level content (what Wealth 3 means vs. Wealth 5) is explicitly not written yet — flagged as new work comparable to filling in the Gift catalog's own level tables. See [perks.md](../rules/perks.md#leveled-resource-perks--confirmed).
- **2026-07-14** — **Compiled the Flaw list**, repeating the same sourcing process used for [premade-skills.md](../rules/premade-skills.md) and [perks.md](../rules/perks.md), but searching each source for disadvantageous traits instead: OWoD Flaws (Merits' direct companion), Hero System Disadvantages/Complications (a strong fit, since Hero's Disadvantages are already built as narrative complications rather than flat penalties), and D&D 3.5e's Unearthed Arcana Flaws (numeric-penalty entries excluded, mirroring how Perks excluded numeric-bonus Feats). Organized into Physical, Mental/Cognitive, Social, and Combat-Adjacent — the last one resolved directly rather than deferred, since combat mechanics already exist now, unlike when Perks' Combat-Adjacent section was first drafted. Several entries were written as deliberate mirrors of existing Perks (Notoriety/Fame, Reduced Reactions/Combat Reflexes & Alertness, Slow Hands/Quick Draw). Left open whether resource-flavored Flaws (Notoriety, Enemy, Hunted) should get the same 1-5 Leveled treatment Perks just got. See [flaws.md](../rules/flaws.md#flaw-list).
- **2026-07-14** — **Addendum: Leveled (Resource) Flaws confirmed**, mirroring Perks — Notoriety, Enemy, and Hunted pulled out of the plain Social list into their own 1-5 scale, same reasoning as Wealth/Contacts/Fame on the Perks side (a scalable liability, not a binary trait). Exact per-level content deferred alongside the Leveled Perks' own gap.
- **2026-07-14** — **Flaw economy confirmed**: Flaws are a character-creation-only transaction — taking one grants extra build points to spend elsewhere, and since there's no mid-campaign mechanism to pick one up or shed one, a chosen Flaw is fixed for the character's lifetime. By symmetry with the "Perk cost scales with strength" decision, the points a Flaw grants should scale with its severity rather than being a flat uniform amount — noted as an inferred mirror rather than a separately confirmed number, since exact figures wait on the point/XP economy either way. See [flaws.md](../rules/flaws.md#resolved).
- **2026-07-14** — **Character creation order and pool structure resolved**: a fixed sequence, Attributes → Skills → Perks → Gifts → Flaws → spend Freebies. The first four each draw from their **own siloed point pool** — an Attribute pool point can't be spent on a Skill, a Gift pool point can't be spent on a Perk, etc. Flaws are taken last among the "acquiring" steps and don't cost anything — they **grant Freebie Points** instead, a fifth and more flexible currency spent in a final pass. Whether Freebies can buy into all four categories (assumed yes, OWoD-style) and their per-category conversion rate are both flagged as open, since the user specified the structure but not the exact numbers. See [character-creation/overview.md](../character-creation/overview.md#creation-order--confirmed).
- **2026-07-14** — **Addendum: introduced Attribute Descriptors**. Every point invested in an Attribute grants one player-chosen Descriptor — a specific flavor of that Attribute for this character (Body 3 might be *Agile, Fast, Durable* rather than three generic points of "strong"). These Descriptors become the concrete tool for arguing a [Skill+Attribute pairing](../rules/skills.md#skills-are-not-attribute-locked): instead of improvising a justification from scratch each time, the player points to an already-established Descriptor. Doesn't remove the GM's final say or the "re-argued every time" rule — it grounds the argument in a pre-chosen character fact rather than a cold improvisation. Left open whether new Descriptors can be added/reassigned without a fresh Attribute purchase. See [core-mechanic.md](../rules/core-mechanic.md#descriptors--confirmed).
- **2026-07-14** — **Point cost confirmed flat 1:1** for all four dedicated creation pools (Attributes, Skills, Perks, Gifts) — 1 pool point buys 1 rank, no scaling curve within a pool. Pool *size* is still open.
- **2026-07-14** — **First calibration pass toward pool sizing**: before setting pool sizes, needed a shared reference point for what a "raw target number" on this system's d20-roll-under scale represents in human terms, since D&D and Hero System both anchor "10" as the average human stat. Computed three conversions: D&D's classic roll-under ability check (identical mechanic to ours — ability 10 converts with zero interpretation to target 10, i.e. 50%), D&D 5e's DC-based check (ability 10 = +0 vs. DC 10 "Easy" → 55%, target ~11), and Hero System's 3d6-under-`9+Char/5` (Characteristic 10 → target 11-, P(3d6≤11) = 135/216 ≈ 62.5%, target ~12.5). Landed on **10 as the recommended baseline target number** — the low end of the resulting 10-13 range, chosen because the classic-D&D comparison is structurally identical to our own mechanic (no die-shape conversion required) and because it has a clean self-consistency property: an average stat of 10 produces a target of 10, so the human baseline number carries straight through unchanged. This is explicitly step 1 of pool-size calibration, not the pool sizes themselves — those come next, informed by the user's stated goal of "above average, with room to grow" (referencing OWoD's starting-Attribute cap as a rough analog). - **2026-07-12** — **Confirmed Dodge mechanic**: multiplies the defender's Degree of Success by 1.5 (round up) on defense rolls this round, rather than a flat bonus. Costs the character's Action; applies only when the defense roll succeeds (has a DoS), not on a failed defense.
